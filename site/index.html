The Ceremony Section I - How a verdict is made

Every dispute runs the same six phases. Each phase is cryptographic, and the final attestation is verifiable by any third party - forever, without asking us.

I

Jury picked

A jury is selected at random for the session. Selection binds identities to the case before anyone votes.

II

Setup - DKG

Jurors run distributed key generation together. The group key is born without any dealer and no single juror holds it.

III

Vote - commit

Each juror commits to a salted hash of their outcome. Nobody can copy anyone else's vote.

IV

Count - reveal

Reveals publish the outcome and salt; each must match the juror's earlier commit or it is rejected.

V

Sign

The jury produces a threshold Schnorr signature over the final verdict - no single juror, and no minority, can forge it.

VI

Verdict

The attestation is published. Anyone can verify it against the Court's group public key.

Guarantees Section II - What the Court never does
⚿

Never holds funds

The Court never holds, receives, redirects, or intermediates user funds. It contains no settlement logic - it authorizes, others settle.

✓

Verifiable by anyone

The verdict is a threshold Schnorr attestation over a group public key. Verification needs no access to the Court, its jurors, or its operators.

◈

No single point of trust

Key generation is distributed, votes are secret until counted, and the verdict requires a threshold of jurors - not an admin key.

Authority layer only The Court decides outcomes and authorizes downstream actions. Escrow, payouts and settlement stay in the protocols and rails that already hold the money.
